Make your first ElevenLabs voiceover.

By Nizar Assad · September 14, 2026 · Based on one short-script trial

Start with a short passage from your project, choose a voice and review the output before generating more. Here’s the workflow I used and what to check before putting the audio in a public video.

1. Enter your script and choose a voice

Open Text to Speech in ElevenLabs. Paste the passage you want to turn into audio, then check the voice and model in the settings panel. For a first attempt, use a short section that includes any names or technical terms your finished video needs.

In my trial, I used Roger — Laid-Back, Casual, Resonant with Eleven Multilingual v2. The script was:

The pipeline uses retrieval-augmented generation, vector embeddings, asynchronous workers, and a FastAPI service behind a reverse proxy.

This walkthrough covers that model and voice. I haven’t evaluated Eleven v3 here. Your interface and available options may differ by plan or change over time.

2. Generate and keep the original

Check your available credits and any generation charge shown before proceeding. Generate the passage, play it back and use the download control to save the original audio. Keep a note of the voice and model so you can return to the same setup.

My session produced two different MP3 outputs, each about nine seconds long. I preferred the take labeled Generation 2. That is a listening preference for this sentence, not a claim that the second take will always be better. I did not measure generation speed or establish a cost per finished minute.

3. Listen for a specific problem

Follow your script while listening. Check whether the output includes every word, handles names correctly and pauses where the meaning calls for a pause. Then decide whether its delivery suits the video.

  • A name sounds wrong: check the spelling and available pronunciation controls before generating again.
  • The delivery feels awkward: identify the phrase or pause that needs attention instead of changing every setting.
  • The take works: save it and move on to editing. Another generation is optional.

My two takes are enough to document this first-use workflow. They are not enough to establish long-form consistency or a general quality ranking.
